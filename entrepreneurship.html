Tech Entrepreneurship

Turn a real problem you understand into something you can actually build and launch.

6–8 weeks · 4–6 hrs/week Some tech skill from another module is a plus 4 Learning Phases
Play learning game → All games

You don't need a computer science degree or investor funding to start building something real — you need a genuine problem, a simple first version, and the discipline to test it with real people. This track walks through the same lean process founders around the world use, adapted for a first-time builder in Pakistan.

Capstone Module Applies Other Skills Real First Users 4 Phases
Mentor TipTalk to ten real potential users before writing a single line of code — it's the cheapest research you'll ever do.

The Roadmap

01

FoundationsFinding a real problem worth solving

The best businesses start from a specific, real problem the founder understands deeply — not a copied idea.

01
What actually makes a startupSolving a real problem, repeatably, for people who will pay or engage.
02
Spotting problems around youLooking at your own school, community, or daily frustrations for ideas.
03
Idea validationTalking to potential users before building anything, to test if the problem is real.
04
Competitive researchUnderstanding who else is solving this, and what you'd do differently.
Practice ProjectInterview five people about a problem you think you've spotted, and write down what you actually learned.
02

Building the BasicsTurning an idea into a plan

A simple, one-page plan keeps you focused far better than a 20-page business plan nobody reads.

01
The lean canvasA one-page framework covering problem, solution, and audience.
02
MVP thinkingDefining the smallest version of your idea that still delivers real value.
03
Basic market researchSizing your potential audience realistically.
04
Naming & positioningDescribing what you do in one clear sentence.
Practice ProjectFill out a lean canvas for your idea and write a one-sentence pitch you could say out loud.
03

Building & TestingMaking your first version real

This is where the idea becomes something people can actually try — even if it's simple.

01
Choosing your MVP formatA website, a simple app, or even a manual service — pick the fastest path to testing.
02
Using the skills you already haveApplying HTML/CSS, design, or Python skills from other modules to build it.
03
Getting your first real usersReaching ten real people willing to try what you've built.
04
Collecting honest feedbackAsking questions that get truthful answers, not just polite ones.
Practice ProjectLaunch a simple landing page or working prototype and get at least 10 people to try it.
04

Growth & PitchingTelling your story and finding support

A great idea still needs to be communicated clearly to grow — this phase covers the storytelling and resources to do that.

01
Basic budgetingUnderstanding costs, even for a free or low-cost first version.
02
Storytelling & pitch decksStructuring a short, compelling pitch anyone can follow.
03
Finding mentorshipWhere young founders in Pakistan can find guidance and community.
04
Funding pathways for young foundersAn honest overview of grants, competitions, and early funding options.
Practice ProjectBuild a 6-slide pitch deck for your idea and present it out loud to at least one other person.
